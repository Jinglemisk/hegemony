# Phase 3.5 closeout campaign

Date: 2026-08-03

## Question

Does the completed Assembly revision make the agora worth engaging, avoid a
no-coup runaway leader, and leave the Low-number economy on a credible victory
clock? Should Polis Builder move from three cities to two, or should politician
prizes be raised?

## Method

- Shuffled boards and a 140-turn ceiling throughout.
- A rotated 24-game Low-number table with two `political` and two `smart` seats,
  seeds 84000–84005, measured final engagement with the three-city minimum.
- Six matched `master` games per arm, seeds 84200–84205, compared Standard,
  Low-number with three cities, and Low-number with two cities.
- A prior rotated 24-game prize sweep compared the accepted Low-number prizes
  (+2 food / +2 stone / +3 wood / +1 happiness) with +3 / +3 / +4 / +2.
- Reports used resolved content hash `ca92aa1d`. Raw JSON was generated as local
  campaign output; this compact record preserves the closeout evidence.

## Final Assembly engagement and runaway check

| Measure | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Finished by victory race / deck / turn cap | 17 / 7 / 0 |
| Political wins | 13/48 seats (27%) |
| Smart wins | 11/48 seats (23%) |
| Directives passed | 3.1/game |
| Authored resolutions passed | 6.4/game |
| Standing Laws at end | 5.7/game (cap 6) |
| Voice held at end | 12/24 games (50%) |
| Voice-holder wins | 8/24 finished games (33%) |
| Mean top-author share | 39% |
| Influence sunk into Assembly | 21.5/game |

The reference political policy participates, and doing so is not a net-loss
strategy. Voice is attainable without becoming a proxy for the winner: its holder
won one third of finished games. The mean authored leader held 39% of passes,
well short of monopolising the four-seat table. The Law cap held under concurrent
proposals after reserved replacement targets were added.

No turn reached the simulator's action cap and no pending decision was force-resolved.

## Polis Builder: three cities versus two

| Low-number `master` arm | Mean / median turns | Victory / deck / cap | Colony→city upgrades |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Three cities | 91.7 / 95 | 6 / 0 / 0 | 0.8/game |
| Two cities | 76.0 / 78 | 6 / 0 / 0 | 1.7/game |

Two cities reduced the matched mean by 17% and made the bot upgrade more often, but
it also awarded Polis Builder immediately after the first routine colony upgrade.
The contrast exposed a policy weakness: `master` has no cross-turn saving objective
and does not value whether a colony is a legal future city site. It strongly follows
the immediate victory-card reward at two cities but does not plan the two-upgrade arc.

**Decision:** keep Polis Builder at three cities in both Standard and the
`low-number-core-v1` preset. Treat the slow city result as an AI-planning follow-up,
not evidence that the card's intended territorial arc should be shortened.

## Prize sweep

The larger prize arm reduced political wins from 27% to 23%, reduced Directives
from 3.1 to 2.1/game, reduced Voice-held endings from 50% to 38%, and increased
the mean top-author share from 39% to 45%. It also introduced one safety-cap turn.

**Decision:** reject the increase. Keep the scarcity-scaled Low-number prizes at
+2 food, +2 stone, +3 wood, and +1 happiness.

## Verdict and evidence limit

The automated Phase 3.5 simulation gates pass: `master` exercises the revised
Assembly, the agora is worth engaging, the Law cap is enforced, the no-coup
runaway indicators are healthy, and the city threshold has an evidence-backed
final value. The owner reviewed the implementation and directed Phase 3.5 to
close; no separate full-game human playtest transcript was recorded, so this
report does not represent one.
