# v1 against v2: what changed and why

Date: 2026-10-19. Code: v1 is `fbc3dc2^1`, `main` just before v2 merged, whose engine and
bots match the 2026-09-05 baseline commit `4a9045a`. v2 is `main` at `fbc3dc2`; the game code
is unchanged through `eecc7c5`.

## Question

v2 games look nothing like v1's. The owner asked why on four counts: slaves are half the
population, gold income is close to zero, realms are often short of food, and games end at the
deck instead of the title race. Happiness, which sits at about −2, came up along the way. The
owner asked to compare the records we already had, not to rerun v1.

## Data

- **v1:** the saved [shallow-economy baseline](2026-09-05-shallow-economy-baseline.md) reports
  (standard mode: 3 games with master, 10 with smart), and the classic/smart figures from the
  [Step 4 report](2026-10-02-v2-step4-bank-and-food.md). Nothing was rerun.
- **v2, rotated:** one new batch, 40 games of 56 turns, the four personalities rotated through
  the seats on seeds 1000 to 1009:
  [report](2026-10-19-v1-vs-v2-rotated.json), [turn snapshots](2026-10-19-v1-vs-v2-rotated.csv).

  ```bash

  ```

- **v2, four masters without Ideas:** figures from the Step 19 and 20 builds and Q80, as

Rotated win rates: slaver 18%, civic 20%, trader 28%, master 35%, each about ±13 points at 40
games. 39 games ended at the deck, 1 by the race.

## 1. Why slaves became half the population

**The rules did it, not the lower numbers or the bot weights.**

- In v1, every settlement collected its tile's printed yield whoever lived there, and a slave
  ate food and cost happiness every turn, so a slave lost money.
- In v2, tiles print nothing: a slave is the yield, eats nothing, and costs only a standing −½
  level. On plains that makes it the best-paying pop.
- The master's weights are the same as v1's smart. Slave Colonies affects only the slaver's
  seat.

In v1 the classic tiles print 44 food, 36 wood and 26 stone in total, paid even with no slaves
(`popIncome`, `GROW_POP_COSTS`, `settlementNetYield`).

Net per turn, valuing food at the bank (buy for 2 gold, sell 3 for 1 gold):

The best pop in v1 is the freeman. In v2 it is a slave on plains, which repays its 2-food price
in 2 turns.

The three causes, separated (measured class shares, except where marked):

- **Lower numbers alone didn't do it.** v1's Lower Numbers preset kept slaves at 7–11%.
- **Rules.** The master's weights are identical to v1's smart: pops slave 1.2, freeman 2,
  citizen 3; materials food 0.4, wood 0.6, stone 0.85, gold 1.
  - v1 masters ended at 6% slaves, v1 smart at 8%.
  - v2 four masters with no Ideas hold about 5 slaves of 13.3 pops, about 38%.
  - In the rotated batch the non-slaver seats are 32–44% slaves, and none of them drafts Slave
    Colonies.
  - So the rules account for about +30 points with the weights unchanged.
- **Weights.** The slaver weighs a slave at 4.5 and ends with 16.6 slaves (70%). Take the
  slaver seats out and the pooled share falls from 50% to 39%.
- **Slave Colonies.** It grants one slave per founding, and the slaver founds 3.35 a
  seat-game. That is at most about 3.4 of its 16.6 slaves, about 3 points pooled (inferred upper
  bound).

## 3. The gold economy

**v2 cut the gold sources. v1 had more sinks, and dearer ones.**

- A v1 freeman made 2 gold on land that fed it.
- A v2 freeman makes 1 gold and eats a food that costs 2 gold to buy, so it loses gold unless
  the realm grows its own food.
- The data back the source story: v2 stocks don't pile up, they hit zero.

Gold sources:

- v1: freeman +2, citizen +2, Marketplace +2 per freeman, bank sales (3:1, 4:1 abundant, 2:1
  scarce), Convoy on a 5-gold stake (pays 5 or 9), cards, 10 starting gold.
- v2: freeman +1, Marketplace freeman +2, citizen 0, bank sales 3:1, Convoy on a 2-gold stake
  (pays 2 or 4), Stratokles prize 2, Treasury Grant 4, cards, 4 starting gold.

Gold sinks:

- v1: bank buy 2–3 a unit, calm 6, grow a citizen 2 g + 9 food, promote to citizen 4, venture
  5, Villa 4, Port 10, riot bribe 6. Votes and the veto were paid in influence.
- v2: bank buy 2 a unit (food included), calm 2, promote to citizen 2, venture 2, Marketplace
  2, Port 4, extra vote 2, riot bribe 3.
- Every v1 price was higher, and v2 added no new kind of gold sink.

A v2 freeman makes 1 gold and eats 1 food. Fed from the bank it nets −1 a turn; with a
Marketplace, 0. Its 3-food growth price (6 gold at bank prices) is never repaid. Fed from the
realm's own food it gives up a 0.33-gold sale and nets +0.67, and that food would also grow a
slave for 2. Fed through the Dole, it nets +1 gold for −3 influence.

What the data say:

- v1 ended with a median 268 gold even after 3.8 bank buys a turn. v2 ends at a median 0.
- The trader, the freeman build, earns about 86 gold a seat-game (6.2 × 13.9). It spends 32.7
  of that on bank food, loses 2.8 pops a seat-game to hunger, and builds 0.15 Marketplaces.
- If sinks were the gap, gold would pile up. It doesn't.

## 4. "29.5% of turns begin short of food"

**These are turns where the stock is already below zero after the turn's income and upkeep.**
It is not a projection. Most shortfalls are 1–3 food, and bots buy their way out of nine in ten.

The telemetry's `foodPurchases` counts a turn when the acting seat's food is below zero in the
turn snapshot. Snapshots are taken after the next seat's income and draw and before it acts.
Income applies upkeep, and only food may go below zero.

- Median 2, mean 2.9, p90 6; 1,886 units short in all.
- By build: trader 205 turns and 778 units (median 3, max 16); civic 187 and 451; master 182 and
  455; slaver 82 and 202.
- None fall in year 1 (seats start with 12 food). At turn start the median seat held 3 gold and

- 1,584 units bought while short: 763 bank buys (1,526 gold) and 821 Doles (2,107 influence).
- 62 turns ended still short, and 143 pops left.
- The remaining 159 or so units came from food gained during the turn: cards, venture payouts,
  prizes (inferred residual).

## 5. From race wins to deck and tiebreak finishes

**Mainly the clock.** Both versions use the same race (three titles at your own turn start),
and the bots value titles the same way in both: 120 each, linear, with no bonus for the third.
v1 races were won late, around 80 to 130 player-turns, while v2 stops at 56. At turn 56, v1
tables were also stuck on two titles.

Measured:

- v1 race wins came at a mean of 96.9 turns (std/smart) and 83 (master).
- Only 1 of 10 std/smart games and 1 of 3 master games ended by turn 56 (at 51 and 55).
- v1 classic/smart race wins came at round 22.8, about 91 turns.
- At v1 season 14 (turn 56), the most titles any std/smart seat held was 2. Three appear only
  from round 20.
- v2, games with a seat on two titles: year 4, 6 of 40; year 8, 21; year 12, 23; year 14, 26.
  A seat on three appears only in years 10–11 (2 games) and 14 (1).
- v2, most titles any seat held in a game: 2 in 31 games, 3 in 3, 1 in 6.

Contributing (inferred):

- Treasurer (30 gold) and Beloved (level 4) are nearly out of reach for three builds. They are
  held at the end in 8 and 6 games.
- Voice became a level that repeals can strip; 63 repeals passed in the batch.
- The reachable titles split by build (Demos to the slaver, Civic Elite to master and civic,
  Voice to civic), so one seat seldom gathers three.

## 6. Beloved, and why the level sits at about −2

**v1's Beloved read a happiness bank that Temples and Odeons filled every turn (minimum +10).
v2's reads a standing level with nothing to bank (minimum 4).**

- v2's level sits at −2 because every build keeps about 5 slaves (−2 to −2.5) against about one
  Temple and under one luxury.
- Bots ride just above the riot line. Nothing in the rules rewards a positive level except
  Beloved and the tiebreak at the deck's end.

The rules:

- v1: sole highest effective happiness, minimum +10, where effective is the bank + 2 per active
  luxury (3 at most). Each turn adds Temple +1, Odeon +2, and +1 per 5 food held (+2 at most);
  it takes −0.5 a slave, −1 for each pop over capacity, and any hunger shortfall. Calm is +3 at
  once. Riots at −5, revolts at −10.
- v2: sole highest level without calm, minimum 4. The level is Temples (one per settlement) + 2
  per luxury − 1 per two slaves − Unrest tokens, +2 if calm was bought this year. Only tokens
  persist. The level is read by the riot check (riot at −3, revolt at −6), Beloved, the
  tiebreak and the logs. No income, price or card scales with a positive level.

Level by term, years 10–14 (acting turns; "rest" is Temples, calm and Law lines, taken as the
residual):

| Build   | Slaves | Tokens | Luxuries | Rest | Level |
| ------- | ------ | ------ | -------- | ---- | ----- |
| Slaver  | −7.2   | −0.6   | +2.2     | +2.0 | −3.6  |
| Civic   | −2.3   | −0.8   | +0.6     | +1.3 | −1.1  |
| Trader  | −2.6   | −0.9   | +1.5     | +1.2 | −0.8  |
| Master  | −2.5   | −0.9   | +0.9     | +1.1 | −1.4  |

Riding the line (level at turn start, from year 2):

- Civic 68%, master 69% and trader 57% of turns sit at −2 or −1. Only 9–12% are at −3 or below,
  and 0.2–1% at 4 or above.
- The slaver is at −3 or below on 73%. It buys calm 7.3 times a seat-game and still riots 3.1
  times.

Why the bots do it (inferred from code): the bots score a level linearly, 6 a point for the
master, capped at 6. A slave scores 12 plus its output, so half a level point (3) is cheaper
than the slave. The bots add slaves until the riot charge, priced from the riot table since Step
19, stops them at −3. For the slaver a slave scores 45 against 2 for the half point, so the riot
itself is its brake.

## What it led to

Owner decisions, 2026-10-19:

- The bank goes, and every settlement makes 1 food
  ([player trade](../../plans/player-trade.md), D9 and D10).
- The [development deck](../../plans/development-deck.md) replaces Ventures and gives gold a
  use. A level of +3 or higher draws a free card, so happiness pays above zero; a riot may take
  a random card instead of a pop.
- Number tuning waits for player trade's slice 2 batches, read against this one.
