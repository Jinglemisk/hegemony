What Imperator: Rome does

The figure is my schematic of Imperator 2.0's screen, drawn from the screenshots under Sources: placement and proportion, not its art.

Where does everything go?

Imperator 2.0 keeps the centre of the screen for the map. Every edge and corner has one job.

  • Top bar: the realm's numbers, left to right, with the date, game speed and menu at its right end.
  • Top-left: the realm's emblem. A vertical strip of icon buttons runs down the left edge, one per realm window (economy, military, characters, technology, religion and so on). Alerts hang from the bar beside the emblem as pennants, in two colours, and a click acts on them.
  • Realm windows (Technology, Military, a character, Diplomacy, Nation Overview) all open at the same place, top-left beside the strip, one at a time.
  • Bottom-left: the selected province, only while something is selected. Its size follows the pick: a two-part panel for your own province, a narrow card for anyone else's.
  • Top-right: the Outliner, a dark list of what needs you (armies, navies, battles, sieges, disloyal characters and provinces, wars, great wonders). A section appears only when it has entries. "Queued Events" sits under it.
  • Bottom-right: map modes and a minimap.
  • Bottom-centre: transient results, such as a battle report.
  • At the cursor: the map's tooltip, e.g. "City of Lavinium in Latium… Terrain: Farmland".
What is the philosophy?

Paradox rebuilt the interface for the free 2.0 "Marius" update (February 2021). Game director Peter Nicholson described the new look as bolder and cleaner: fewer decorative corners and borders taking up screen space, decoration kept for drawing attention, signalling importance or adding immersion, and airier windows with room for art. The update also brought Crusader Kings III's nested tooltips.

The screenshots add four habits:

  • The map owns the centre.
  • Each corner has one job.
  • A panel appears for a reason and leaves when the reason is gone.
  • Every window shares one frame: a coloured title bar with a Greek key band, a close button, section headers in the same band, and flat content on parchment.
What we take, and what we leave

Take: the corner jobs, a selection panel that appears and resizes with the pick, realm windows opened from a left rail, the bar's stock-over-change cells, map modes that change the picture, nested tooltips, and one frame for every panel.

Take, short: the Outliner. Ours lists a dozen places, the open offers and the Assembly, not hundreds of armies, and you choose what it shows. Leave: the minimap, since one island fits the opening view and the compass already brings it back. Leave the red-and-white Roman colouring and the portraits too: our frame stays ink, bone and ivory, with the bronze icons.

The overhaul

Proposal B

A copy of the Proposal with four changes. Everything below describes the Proposal; where B differs, these points stand in its place.

  • The dock. The log line runs across the whole dock, End turn included. End turn sits under it, beside the map modes and Trade, Buy a card and the Dole, and is 126 px tall instead of 162.
  • The rail is a tab centred on the left edge, with buttons 56 by 50 and 33 px icons. It rises to stay clear of the inspector, and when a drawer opens it runs the drawer's height with its buttons where they were.
  • Drawers are 320 px wide, the outliner's width, so the two edges hold the same width. The inspector keeps its own width beside a drawer, so the hand's fixed place moves to 560 px, right of the large inspector, and widens to 544 px.
  • Drawer contents follow Imperator's panels: tabs are equal buttons across the drawer, sections open under a dark band, and figures are icon tiles. Rules and effects moved into tooltips. Cities is one two-line row a place, People three class columns, Ideas tiles with free ones first, Agora four stelae and the orators' grid.

The corner: the inspector

What you do to a place, you do from the place. Nothing picked: the corner is map. On a pick, the inspector slides in from the left edge at its own height. Its Grow and Raise options pop to its right, level with its foot.

  • L, your settlement (540 px): people out of room, slots used, what it makes a turn. Below that, two columns: slot squares on the left (built, worked, open; an open slot raises a building) and the three classes on the right, each with what it makes here and ▲ ▼ to promote or demote. The footer holds Grow here, Raise a building (or Make it a city) and Move a pop out, each with its cost and shortfall.
  • M, a rival's settlement (540 px): the same facts without the controls, and one action, "Offer Nikos a trade", which opens the composer with Nikos chosen. The owner's icon in the title opens their card beside the inspector: people, places, happiness, gold, titles, Assembly, cards, Ideas.
  • With a drawer open, any size widens to the drawer's edge (704 px), and the drawer's foot meets the inspector's top.
  • S, land and the Oracle (360 px): what the land is, what it would give, and the one action it allows: Found a colony here.

Rules it keeps.

  • It opens on a pick. Esc, ×, a click on the sea, or another pick changes or closes it.
  • It is flush to the screen's edges, never floating.
  • A pick shows twice: a lit outline on the tile, and the inspector's title.
  • If any panel would cover the picked tile, the camera eases the tile clear.
  • It never scrolls. A settlement with more to say grows the corner upward.
  • A disabled action names its shortfall: "1 stone short", never "Not enough resources".

The dock

  • Order, top to bottom: the chronicle's last line, the map modes, the three buttons. End turn spans all three rows on the right.
  • Map modes are square framed buttons with no names, as in Imperator. The tooltip names the mode and its key (1–4), and the legend above the dock says what the mode shows.
  • The chronicle shows its last line. A click opens the full log upward out of that line at the dock's width, oldest at the top and the newest next to the line. The outliner shortens to make room. The line, ×, or Esc closes it.
  • Trade, Buy a card, the Dole sit side by side, each with its cost under its name (the Dole shows 3 influence → 1 food). Trade opens the composer, with the picked rival chosen if there is one. Buy a card and the Dole ask first.
  • End turn spans the three rows. Hold to end, as today.

Are you sure?

  • Every action that spends asks once: Buy a card, the Dole, Calm, grow, raise, promote and demote, found, make it a city, a second Idea, and the Assembly's draw and repeal.
  • One component for all of them: a wide, quiet bar over the centre, the board dimmed only a little. "Are you sure you want to promote a freeman to citizen in Samos? This will cost 2 gold: gold 2 → 0. Up to three ladder moves a turn." Cancel, and the action's own verb.
  • Enter says yes, Esc or a click outside says no. An action you can't pay for never reaches it: its button names the shortfall.

Drawers and menu windows

  • Drawers are the windows about your realm, all 640 px wide. One is open at a time, opened from the rail, or from the seal for Realm. They slide out of the rail, take the full height, and shorten when the inspector opens. A row in Cities picks the place without closing the drawer.
  • Realm (Overview, Cities, People) shows no figure the top bar already shows. Cities has a Next column: what each place is short of. People is the ladder as a table with each move's price. Ideas is the draft's eleven cards with their holders. Agora shows the next sitting, the standing Laws side by side on their stelae (the Assembly sheet's cards), and the orators by seat.
  • Menu windows are what you consult or set while you play: Victory, Codex, Costs, Plates. Each opens top-right, moves by its title bar, stays inside the screen, and comes to the front on a click. Esc closes the one on top. The menu marks the ones already open.
  • Victory leads with the threat when a seat is on three titles. Codex is a two-pane reader with search, holding all fifteen chapters. Costs is the price card in two columns: red marks what today's store cannot pay, and a tinted row is moved by a standing Law. Plates holds the badge settings.

The outliner

  • A dark glass list down the right edge, as in Imperator, but short, since ours has a dozen places, not hundreds of armies.
  • Your settlements: one row each, with people over room and the next thing it needs ("Empty: grow or move a pop in", "A Port claims Tyrian Dye"). A row picks the place, and the camera eases it clear.
  • Trade: open offers first, then the luxury goods and who holds each, then the free ones beside you. An offer's row opens its card.
  • The Assembly: the next sitting, your votes, the standing Laws and their authors. A row opens the Agora drawer.
  • Settings (the gear): which sections show, rivals' settlements included, and the order of your places (needs you first, by people, A–Z). Every section folds. The dash tucks the whole outliner into a tab on the edge.

The hand

  • Held cards peek from the bottom edge, in one fixed place between the corner's widest and the dock, and rise when you hover them. A click, or H, opens the hand.
  • The hand view lays the cards out full size. Each card has Play, or the engine's reason it can't be played ("A card is never played on the turn it came in", "You have no Unrest token to clear"). Playing asks for the card's choice in place: a settlement with room for Settlers, a rival and a kind for Raiders.
  • Under the cards, three facts the player otherwise has to know: buying is 2 gold once a turn, a free card comes at +3 happiness, and a Riot or an Informer can take a held card.
  • A new card shows "plays from your next turn" on its face. The table sees how many cards you hold, never which, as it does today.

Moments

  • These keep today's components and copy. The shell only decides where each one sits.
  • Assembly: a sheet over the map with the stage rail (Proposal, Ballot, Vote, Rises). Minimise sends it to a pill under the alarms, so a player can check the board and the outliner before sealing.
  • An offer from a rival lands as a disc on the alarms row and as the first row of the outliner's Trade section. Its card opens under the seal and shows what you would hold after accepting.
  • Riot: the warning opens above End turn, where the press happened. The sheet keeps today's insurance and table. Hunger keeps today's card.
  • A card drawn keeps today's reveal: a yield pays at once ("Take the wood"), any other card goes to the hand ("Keep it").

Map modes

  • Each mode must be recognisable from a thumbnail. Political paints owned land flat in the realm's glaze and writes the realm's name large across its land. Terrain keeps today's look.
  • A short legend sits above the dock in Political, Economy and Trade, built from the descriptions the modes already carry in code. It rides up with the chronicle when that opens.
  • Keys 1–4 stay as they are.

Tooltips

  • A tooltip shows the cause behind a figure: each stock itemises its change by settlement, and happiness lists what raises and lowers it.
  • A tooltip with underlined words locks when you rest on it. A bar along its foot fills in 0.6 s, then the pointer can move into it, and an underlined word opens its own tooltip beside it. That one can nest again. Leaving them all closes the stack after 0.3 s.
  • The words come from one glossary in the shell, worded to match the Codex, so each rule is written once: happiness, Riot, revolt, Calm, Unrest token, luxury good, Port, the three classes, influence, titles, Beloved of the People.

Standards the shell meets

  • Text at 1440×900: names 20–22 px, body 14–15 px, small-caps labels at least 10 px. Numbers use tabular figures.
  • Targets: map-mode squares 46 px, rail buttons 54×44 px, dock buttons 77×66 px, End turn 88×162 px; everything else at least 32 px.
  • Every panel is flush to an edge or to another panel, except the menu windows, which you place. Every panel has a title, a close button and Esc. Esc closes the top-most thing: the confirm, card, menu, pop, alarm or player card, chronicle, the window on top, then the pick.
  • No text overprints. No panel hides another's controls, unless you have put it on top. The geometry audit (ui:audit) gates this at 1280, 1440 and 1920 wide.
  • Every number that changes shows its change, coloured by whether it helps or hurts.
  • Panels move in about 240 ms (drawers, inspector, chronicle, outliner and hand together), and motion stops under reduced motion.
  • An empty state hides, or says what would fill it: "No cards held · Buy one in the dock".

Build order

This is UI work in the shell. The engine, the projections and the 3D map's renderer don't change; in slice 7 the map changes only its mode filter and labels. Each slice is one PR that leaves the game playable.

  1. The corner. Take the subject page out of the realm panel into a docked inspector with three sizes, carrying Grow, the ladder, Move a pop, Found, Make it a city and Raise, and widening to an open drawer's edge. The verb discs go. Build the confirm here, and route every spending action through it. Add the pick's outline on the tile and the shortfall wording. Today's settlement, tile, Oracle and good pages are reused inside it.
  2. Drawers. Today's overview, cities and ladder pages move into one Realm drawer, joined by Ideas and Agora, opened from the rail and the seal and sized to the inspector. The Build page folds into the inspector's open slots and the Build fan. The realm panel is then gone.
  3. Menu and its windows. Victory, Codex and Costs leave the top bar for the menu, joined by Plates from the dock, and open as movable windows.
  4. Outliner. Three sections and its settings, built from data the realm panel already reads.
  5. Hand. The tray in its fixed place on the bottom edge and the hand view, reusing today's card faces, hand modal and play choices.
  6. Top-right, top bar and tooltips. The seat discs go, and a rival's icon in the inspector opens their card. Change sits under each stock, and tooltips itemise it by settlement. Tooltips lock on a rest and open their terms from one glossary. The happiness readout opens its card, with Calm.
  7. The dock and map modes. The dock becomes three rows: the chronicle opening from its last line, square map modes, Trade, Buy a card and the Dole with their costs, End turn spanning them. Political's flat paint and large names, the legend, and camera clearance on a pick.
  8. Moments, re-anchored. The Assembly's minimise pill, alarm cards under the seal, the riot warning above End turn. Their contents stay as they are.

Each slice ends with screenshots at 1280, 1440 and 1920, and with ui:audit and ui:conduct green.
