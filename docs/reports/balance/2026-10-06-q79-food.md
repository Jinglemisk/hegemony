# Q79: should settlements eat, and should food be a level?

Status: adopted. The owner took the recommendation (option E) on 2026-10-08; the ruling is in the [v2 migration plan](../../plans/v2-migration.md)'s Settled inputs and Step 17 builds it

Research note, 2026-10-06. Code: `feat/v2` at 2b84b77, prototyped in a throwaway worktree
(now removed). Five 40-game rotated batches (slaver, civic, trader, master; seeds 1000–1009 ×
4 rotations; 56-turn cap, never reached) plus an 8-game check of the owner's example numbers.

## Answer

Keep free pops as the mouths (no to settlements eating), and keep food as a stock (no to a
pure level). Take one small piece of the level idea: **check hunger at the end of the
player's own turn, beside the riot check, instead of at income.** Food stays banked, but a
shortfall shows on the board during your turn and you can cover it (bank, Dole, Granary)
before you end the turn; if you don't, one pop of your choice leaves per missing food.

In the sims this cuts pops lost to hunger by three quarters (2.73 → 0.64 per seat-game),
leaves expansion where it is (2.7 colonies founded per seat-game in both), and needs no new
content. Settlements eating, at any size the current board can feed, moves the food burden
onto the slave economy: slaver wins fall from 32% to 8–10% and colony founding falls by a
third to a half.

## 1. Two switches, not one

Q79 bundles two independent changes:

|                       | Food is a stock | Food is a level |
| --------------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| **Free pops eat**     | A (today)       | D               |
| **Settlements eat**   | B               | C               |

"Who eats" changes the balance between builds. "Stock or level" changes what food can pay
for. They have different costs and different evidence, so they are judged separately below.

Food budget over a game, per seat (rounded):

- **In:** about 66 from plains slaves and Granaries, 12 starting food, 5 bought at the bank,
  7 from the Dole, 3 from Good Stores, about 4 from Colonists' Voyage.
- **Out:** about 61 eaten by free pops; about 30 spent as a **price**: growing slaves (10)
  and freemen (11.5), promoting slaves to freemen (4), founding (2.7), paid moves (1.5);
  2 lost to Rats.

So a third of all food is a currency, not upkeep. That matters for option C.

Hunger today: 1.24 hunger turns and 2.73 pops lost per seat-game (trader 4.7, master 3.3,
slaver 1.5, civic 1.4). It lands at income, at the start of the turn, so a player
cannot answer a loss that came between turns (the year card, a riot, the Assembly).

## 3. The options

### A. Today

- **Board:** food stock in the bar; the hunger alarm shows when next income runs short.
- **Sources and sinks:** as listed above. Nothing changes.
- **Hunger:** at income, one pop per unfed mouth, freemen or citizens (Step 13: player picks).
  It self-corrects: a pop that leaves stops eating.

### B. Settlements eat, food stays banked

- **Board:** each settlement prints its upkeep; the stock stays.
- **Numbers:** the owner's example (colony 1, city 4) asks for 6.4 food a turn in year 1 and
  8.7 in year 8 at today's settlement counts, against 2.4 and 5.2 produced. It cannot be fed
  on the current slot table. In an 8-game check, 25 of 32 realms ended the game with two pops
  or fewer. **Colony 1, city 2** matches today's total demand (year 8: 5.8 against 4.4 mouths
  today) and is what was tested.
- **Sources and sinks:** unchanged (Granary, Dole, bank, cards, starting food all keep their
  jobs).
- **Hunger:** one pop per missing food, and now any pop can leave, slaves included, or a
  slave realm never pays. It **does not self-correct**: the settlement keeps eating after its
  pops leave. Four of the slaver's 40 realms ended at 0–1 pops while their empty colonies still
  ate. A fix would need one more rule (an empty settlement eats nothing, or an unfed colony is
  abandoned).
- **Build effect:** the paper's two brakes (section 5.1: slaves pay in unrest, free pops pay
  in food) collapse into one. The slave economy now pays both; freemen and citizens become
  free to feed.

### C. Settlements eat, food is a level

As written, food is never stored. That breaks every food **price**, which the question's list
leaves out: growing a slave (2) or freeman (3), promoting a slave (2), the paid move (1) and
founding (1) all need a new currency, about 30 food per seat-game today. On top of that the
Dole, bank food trades, starting food, Good Stores, Rats, the Colonists' Voyage payout,
Demosthenes's prize, the riot table's food row, the bread-dole insurance, Grain Riot,
Festival Calendar and Colonial Charter each need a new job. The Granary, Good Harvest, Sacred
Fields, Land Reform, Grain Levy and Drought work unchanged as terms of the level.

The cheapest version that keeps all of those is a **harvest**: the granary holds only this
turn's food. Spend it or sell it this turn; it empties at turn end, and a granary below zero
at turn end is hunger. That is what was simulated as C (and as D, with pops eating).
Option C as written, with a new growth currency, was not simulated: picking that currency is
a design decision, not a dial.

### D. Pops eat, food is a harvest

The level without the upkeep switch. Board reads one number, like happiness. Same content
as today; the stock rule and the hunger timing change.

### E. Hybrid: today's rule, hunger checked at turn end (recommended)

- **Board:** food stays a stock. Income can take it below zero during your own turn; the bar
  shows the shortfall in red with the same end-of-turn warning the riot line has.
- **Sources and sinks:** all unchanged. Starting food, the Dole, bank trades and the ±2 cards
  keep their jobs, and gain a sharper one: covering a shortfall you can see.
- **Hunger:** at your turn end, before the riot check, one pop of your choice leaves per
  missing food, and food returns to zero. It still self-corrects.
- **New rules:** one (the timing). Food and happiness then resolve at the same moment.

What the numbers say:

- **Settlements eating (B, C) does what the owner predicted, and too much of it.** Colonies
  stop being cheap: founding falls 47% under B and 32% under C, and the trader nearly stops
  expanding (0.1 and 0.7 colonies a game). Free pops become free, so citizens double (2.6 →
  5.7–5.9) and the trader holds Treasurer at the end of 39 of 40 games in both. The slave
  economy, which had no food problem by design, now carries it: slaver wins fall to 10% and
  8%, below Step 11's 20% floor. Under C the master, which balances all three classes, takes
  52%.
- **The owner's example size cannot be fed.** Colony 1, city 4 left 25 of 32 realms at two
  pops or fewer. Making it work needs roughly double today's food output: the plains slot
  table up again, or the Granary at +4.
- **Most of today's hunger is timing, not shortage.** E changes only when hunger is checked
  and cuts pops lost from 2.73 to 0.64; bots buy a little more food (14.8 against 12.1 a
  game) because they can now see the shortfall in their own turn. That matches Step 4's
  finding that 97% of hunger events were fixable and 92% were not visible a turn earlier.
- **A harvest (D) is worse than a turn-end check (E).** It also moves hunger to turn end,
  but throwing away the stock cuts less hunger (1.57) and the bots stop building Granaries
  (0.09 a game). Civic wins drop to 15%. The level gives a cleaner reading but no gain the
  turn-end check doesn't already give.

Bot play: no scorer fix was made in any arm. Bots used the new rules sensibly in the main:
they bought food in their turn under E, sold surplus under the harvest (C, D: 0.9–1.3 food
sales a game against 0.3 today), and built Granaries five times as often when settlements ate.
Two weak spots. Under B and C bots may under-value a plains colony that feeds itself, which
could exaggerate the founding drop. Under the harvest the scorer sees no lasting value in a
Granary, so the D Granary figure is partly a bot gap.

## 5. Recommendation and cost

**Q79 answer:** keep option A's rule, plus E's timing: hunger is checked at the end of the
player's own turn, before the riot check.

Cost of E:

- **Engine:** income stops applying hunger. Food may sit below zero only during its owner's
  turn; turn end settles it (one pop per missing food, then zero). Card and table losses at
  income must floor at "the lower of zero and the current stock", so a loss never raises a
  negative stock. The food invariant becomes "never below zero outside the owner's turn". Small:
  the prototype was about 20 lines across income, turn end and the invariant.
- **Shell:** the food figure shows a red shortfall during your turn; the hunger alarm reads
  "N short at turn end" instead of "next income". End turn warns about it like the riot line.
- **Bots:** the projection settles hunger at turn end instead of at income (about 10 lines,
  already prototyped); the food reserve logic stays.
- **The Step 13 hunger card:** same card, same pick (freemen preselected, slaves not
  offered). It opens when the player ends a turn short of food instead of at turn start, and
  it can offer "go back" so the player can still buy food. It sits beside the riot ceremony
  at the same moment.
- **Docs:** `rules.md` hunger section; the Step 3 default "hunger at income, no grace" is
  replaced.

Cost of the rejected options, for comparison:

- **B:** one upkeep line per settlement, hunger able to take slaves (the Step 13 card must
  offer them), a rule for empty settlements, and a retune of the slaver build.
- **C as written:** all of B, a new currency for growth, promotion, moves and founding, about
  a dozen content rows rewritten, and the bots' food reserve replaced.

## 6. Risks

- **E softens the food brake.** Hunger becomes rare (0.29 hunger turns a seat-game), and pops
  end about two higher. Growth prices still make food the brake on size; if Step 11 shows free
  pops running away, raise the freeman price before touching who eats.
- **Win rates move, within noise.** Under E slaver 42%, civic 25%, trader 5% (2 of 40),
  master 28%. One 40-game rotation has a standard error of about 7 points a personality. The
  trader number is worth watching in Step 11.
- **A negative stock on the bar** is a new thing for a player to read. It needs the same
  clarity as the riot warning, or it will look like debt.
- **Riots tick up slightly** (1.60 → 1.95 a game), from more slaves surviving.
- If the owner still wants expansion to cost food, the one-number version is a higher
  founding price (colony 1 food today), not a standing upkeep.

## Method and caveats

- Prototype switches: a per-settlement food upkeep, a harvest flag (granary empties at turn
  end; shortfall at turn end is hunger) and a turn-end hunger flag, each set by a ruleset
  patch. Free pops' food was set to zero when settlements ate. Under settlement upkeep hunger
  removed freemen, then citizens, then slaves, each from the largest holding.
- In the prototypes food could sit below zero across the Assembly and the riot table (the
  floor was lowered to allow a turn-end shortfall). A Grain Riot or the riot table's food row
  could therefore carry a small shortfall into the next turn. Riots were 1.5–2 a game, so the
  effect is small; a real build should floor those losses at zero.
- Win rates and riot counts from one 40-game rotation are noisy; the hunger and founding
  differences are large enough to stand.
- The baseline A was rerun on the same commit and seeds; it differs from Step 10's report
  only by later changes on `feat/v2`.
