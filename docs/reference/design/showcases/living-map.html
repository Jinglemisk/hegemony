Legend

    The island is the engine's classic layout: 37 hexes with their real terrain and slots, and the six goods at one of the engine's even spacings. The settlements are an invented game that follows the player guide: cities never adjacent, colonies at 4 pops or fewer, Ports only on the coast, first Port wins. Slots, yields and happiness are counted with the guide's rules. Trees, rocks, fields and walking paths come from the board and seed 42, so the same state always looks the same; one figure stands for each pop.

    Course of action

    Build the living map as its own track behind a board flag, one PR per slice. Every slice is a pure function of engine state, the camera and the match seed, so balance work never waits on it and cannot be broken by it.

    1. Board shell. Prisms, sea, camera, picking, hidden tile buttons, HTML name plates. Reads tiles (q, r, terrain, slots) and each settlement's kind, owner and name. Done when the smoke test and both UI audits pass on the 3D board.
    2. Zoom bands. One band function of the camera drives every layer's fade and the HUD. Far: owner wash, seals and names. Middle: slots and yield chips. Close: the living detail. View state only; reads nothing new.
    3. Settlements, Ports and goods. Reads settlement buildings, isCoastalTile, G.board.luxuries and their owners.
    4. Slot nodes. Reads settlementSlots, occupiedBuildingSlots and settlementWorkingSlaves, the selectors behind today's pips, so the map cannot disagree with the rules.
    5. Terrain and clearing. Seeded resource volume follows printed slots, or open slots in a town. Permanent points keep surviving trees in place as buildings claim ground.
    6. Pops. One figure per pop from settlement pops; ships carry G.transfers.
    7. Map modes. Political reads owners. Economy reads tileSlaveColumn and settlementNetYield, the ledger's own numbers. Unrest reads happinessContributions, unrestTokens and the ruleset's riot and revolt lines. Trade reads luxuries, Ports, claimableLuxuriesAt and transfers.
    8. Sea life, then the switch. Cosmetic ships, gulls and smoke from seed and clock. Make the 3D board the default and retire the SVG board.

    Approve first: the band edges and what each band shows; the five modes and their keys; the town grammar (buildings face paths, slaves work at resource edges, exact slot counts stay on Economy chips); the three pop silhouettes; and the clearing rule (irregular towns with resources proportional to open slots).

    Risks. Readability: the bands are the guard, and nothing outlined may sit below the middle band. Performance: measured on an M2 only; integrated graphics are untested, and figures are capped by the 8-pop city limit. Hidden tile layer: its buttons must follow every camera move and name slots, pops and the active mode's number, or screen readers lose what the map shows. Tests and audits: jsdom cannot draw WebGL, so selectors and the decor function carry unit tests and Playwright reads the DOM layer.
